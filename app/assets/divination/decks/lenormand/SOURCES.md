# Sources for the Lenormand cards

## The deck

A Petit Lenormand of thirty-six cards with playing-card inserts and French
divinatory verses, published by **Geûens-Seaux of Bruges** (the imprint is
on card 27, the Letter: "Déposé en France & en Belgique, Geûens-Seaux,
Bruges"). The Bibliothèque nationale de France dates it between 1895 and
1901 (Gallica, ark:/12148/btv1b10529584r; "Jeu de cartes divinatoire, dit
'Petit Lenormand'", formerly owned by Henry-René d'Allemagne, 1863–1950).
The designer is not recorded.

The pictures follow the standard Lenormand of thirty-six cards (Rider,
Clover, Ship … Cross), numbered and named as in J. K. Hechtel's
*Das Spiel der Hofnung* of about 1799, each with the usual playing card in
its corner.

## Files

Downloaded on 26 October 2026 from Wikimedia Commons, category
"Jeu de cartes divinatoire, dit "Petit Lenormand" - estampe - btv1b10529584r",
directly from `upload.wikimedia.org`, with a delay between requests and a
descriptive User-Agent. The fronts are the odd-numbered scans, 1 of 74 to
71 of 74 (card *n* is scan 2*n* − 1; the even scans are the backs, 73 and
74 the instruction sheet and box). Because the full-size originals were
rate-limited, each was taken as Wikimedia's own 960-pixel-wide rendering of
the original:

Every card was checked by eye against its number and name.

`app/scripts/build-lenormand-assets.py` trims the scanner's margin, writes
the website's copies (`assets/divination/lenormand/<id>.webp`, 600 px tall)
and packs the app's copies on two sheets here (`sheet-1.webp`,
`sheet-2.webp`, six across and three down). The downloads themselves are
not kept in the repository.

## Meanings

Written for Salt & Sovereignty after the instruction sheets printed with
German Lenormand decks from the 1840s on and the readings most people use
today (subject and modifier, the houses of the Grand Tableau). Timings are
modern and readers differ on them.
