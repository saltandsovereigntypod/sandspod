# Sources for the Kipper cards

## No pictures

The Kipper cards first appeared about 1890 in Munich (from the stationer and
publisher Matthias Seidlein, by most accounts) and were reprinted unchanged
around 1900 and 1910. In October 2026 no complete scan of an edition old
enough to be in the public domain could be found in an open collection:

- Wikimedia Commons ("Category:Kipper Cards") holds only photographs of
  modern printings, under CC BY-SA, and not a whole deck.
- bavarikon (Historischer Verein Neuburg an der Donau) shows nine cards of
  a thirty-six-card set.
- Europeana, the Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek and Gallica returned no
  complete early Kipper deck; Europeana's one Kipper deck (Spielzeugmuseum
  Nürnberg) is from 1977.

So that nothing partial or doubtful ships, the app and the website draw each
Kipper card themselves, in words: its number, its English name and its
German name (`app/src/components/divination/oracle/OracleFaces.tsx`,
`practice/hedge/oracle.js`). Should a complete public-domain set turn up,
its pictures can be added as the Lenormand ones were.

## Meanings

Written for Salt & Sovereignty after the traditional German readings of
the thirty-six cards (main persons, the house, the living room and the
rest), and the way they are read near and far from the main person.
